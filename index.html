What it does

Everything you need to plan a serious retirement.

Most retirement calculators give you a single number. Retirology models the whole arc — saving, then drawing down every account — with the taxes, healthcare and penalties that decide whether an early retirement works, and tells you what to change.

Mission Control

One screen with where you stand: progress to your FI target, the date your savings get there and whether that's ahead of schedule, your Monte Carlo odds against your goal, and personalized checks on what to fix first.

Optimize my plan New

Your Roth ladder, ACA income target and SEPP choice affect each other, so tuning them one at a time rarely finds the best mix. One click tries them together, keeps whatever leaves you the most after tax, and explains every change in dollars.

Every state's 2026 tax rules

All 50 states and DC under 2026 law, including the breaks most states give retirees: retirement-income exclusions by age, income-tested Social Security and capital-gains breaks — explained in plain English. See every state.

Roth ladders and ACA subsidies

Four conversion modes — Auto-fill to your ACA cap, Spend-driven, Zero-tax and Manual — with each conversion's 5-year clock tracked. MAGI is held under the 2026 subsidy cliff, and 72(t) SEPP and the Rule of 55 cover the years before 59½. How ladders work.

Year-by-year drawdown

Every account tracked on its own, drawn in the order you choose, through federal and state tax, the 3.8% NIIT, early-withdrawal penalties, RMDs and Medicare IRMAA — across all four filing statuses, including survivor years. Totals are in today's dollars.

Social Security, done properly

Your birth year's exact full retirement age, what each claim age pays, spousal benefits, and the survivor rule when one of you dies first — with inheritance and the right filing status afterwards.

Path to FI, from your real budget

Per-account growth and contributions (with 2026 limits and catch-ups), employer match and HSA. Your FI target comes from a category-by-category budget and a simulation of your actual plan — not a guessed round number.

Budget analysis

Paycheck and recurring expenses by category, your real savings rate, and how many years until you reach FI — plus a schedule for the changes ahead, like a paid-off mortgage or kids leaving home.

Monte Carlo and stress tests

Run the whole plan — taxes and all — through hundreds of random or historical market sequences (1928–2024 data), see bad-luck, typical and good-luck outcomes, and replay retiring in 1929, 1973, 2000, 2008 and 2022.

Cash flow you can see

Trace every dollar of any year — from accounts and Social Security, through taxes and healthcare, to what you actually spend. Scrub through the years from your first working year to your last.

Compare scenarios

Save plans and put any two side by side — retire at 46 or 50, claim at 62 or 70, stay or move states — with the differences highlighted, from FI date to lifetime tax and after-tax money left.

Set up in minutes

A short wizard — about you, your spouse, accounts, spending, strategies — gets a personalized plan in a couple of minutes. Defaults are sensible and everything is editable later, down to each account's dividend yield.

Questions

Things people reasonably want to know.

What's the difference between $19 and $65?

Nothing in the software. The app and every feature are identical regardless of tier. The price you choose is what funds the next release. $19 is the floor; $65 is what we suggest if it's not a stretch; $200 is for folks who want to back the project.

Is this a subscription?

No. You pay once and own it. Every update after that — bug-fix patches, new features, and the annual releases with refreshed tax tables — is free, forever. There's no renewal, no per-year charge, nothing to cancel.

Does it handle my state's taxes?

Yes — all 50 states and DC under 2026 law, including the rules that matter to retirees: retirement-income exclusions by age, how Social Security is taxed, capital-gains breaks, and rate changes already passed for later years. Local income taxes like New York City's are included too. See how your state taxes retirees.

Does it connect to my bank or brokerage?

No, on purpose. You type in your balances — a few minutes, a few times a year — and no financial logins or account data ever leave your computer.

What happens to my data?

Stays on your machine in a single SQLite file. We don't see it, can't read it, and have no way to. See the privacy section or the full privacy statement for the per-OS file paths.

Do I pay again for new versions?

Never. Each year's release refreshes the tax tables and adds new features, and it's free for everyone who's bought Retirology — no upgrade fee, no discount code to hunt for. When a new version is out, the app points you to your account, where you re-download the latest build at no charge.

What if Retirology shuts down?

The app keeps working. There are no servers it depends on — the only network call is the once-per-launch update check, and if that fails the app just continues. Your data lives in a local SQLite file you own, so nothing remote is required for the app to function — and your copy keeps running indefinitely.

What does first launch look like?

macOS: the app is signed and Apple-notarized — you'll see the standard one-time "downloaded from the Internet, are you sure?" prompt the first time you open it. Click Open and you're in. Windows: SmartScreen flags installers without an established reputation — click More info → Run anyway. (An EV signing cert is on the post-launch roadmap.) Linux: chmod +x the AppImage and run it.

Why no free version?

A modest one-time price keeps the project sustainable without ads, accounts, subscriptions, or upsells — which is the whole point. The $19 floor is intentionally low; if it's genuinely out of reach, email contact@retirology.app and we'll work something out.
